01 · The operating constraint

The estimate was spread across tools.

While selling, designing, and specifying architectural lighting, I repeatedly encountered the same problem: measurements lived in PDF tools, product rules in manufacturer documents, calculations elsewhere, and the material list in a spreadsheet or estimating system.

Connecting those inputs depended on manual transfers and the estimator’s product knowledge. I set out to carry the same project data through measurement, configuration, and material preparation.

03 · Decisions I specified

Make product rules explicit.

The light source determines electrical compatibility. A driver may support a control protocol that the selected light source does not. I specified the light source as the authority for its voltage and supported protocols. Driver selection must respect those requirements and the connected load.

Environment and assembly method stay separate. A dry-location fixture may be field-built or factory-built. I modeled these characteristics independently so the system can preserve valid choices and distinguish their configuration and ordering requirements.

Real manufacturer catalog and pricing documents inform the relationships among light sources, channels, lenses, mounting, finishes, power supplies, and accessories. Catalog coverage and ordering rules continue to be developed and validated.

04 · Accountable execution

Define the work and how it gets approved.

I defined product requirements, data relationships, compatibility rules, acceptance criteria, and failure conditions. AI tools supported planning, implementation, testing, and review. I retained ownership of product scope, domain validation, manual QA, and final approval.

I formalized the delivery process as Loop Engineering V1, with defined roles for planning, implementation, scope auditing, code review, evidence collection, QA, and approval.
